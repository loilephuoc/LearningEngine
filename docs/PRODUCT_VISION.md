# Product Vision — Desktop Better Than Android

## North star

Learning Engine 2.0 helps a learner focus, retrieve, understand, and remember. It combines a
correct adaptive engine with a calm, media-rich Desktop experience that makes daily learning
feel intentional rather than repetitive.

## Focused learning

The primary surface always makes the next meaningful action obvious. Content management,
authoring, diagnostics, and advanced configuration stay outside the active review rhythm.
Learners may shape scope and mode before a session without taking ownership away from the
session engine.

## Flow

Starting, revealing, rating, pausing, resuming, and completing remain fast and deterministic.
Language-learning media can create listening flow through explicit playback plans, but media
never rates, schedules, or mutates a queue. Every automatic sequence is visible, interruptible,
and recoverable.

## Delight

Delight means responsive transitions, legible content, reliable audio, meaningful feedback,
and a sense of progress—not visual noise. The Desktop should retain Android's immediacy while
using screen space for hierarchy, context, accessibility, and reduced interaction cost.

## Customization

Expose learner choices progressively: content scope, study preset, audio sequence, loop delay,
voice, text/image visibility, and shortcuts. Defaults must work without configuration.
Preferences belong in typed Desktop configuration unless they change durable learning
semantics, in which case they require an application/domain contract.

## Ethical habit formation

- Goals inform; they do not shame.
- No forced streaks, artificial scarcity, surprise autoplay, or hidden penalties.
- Automation never fabricates retrieval evidence.
- Notifications and future reminders are opt-in and easy to disable.
- Progress language reflects learning evidence, not engagement time alone.

## Better-than-Android promise

Preserve Android's strongest product ideas—rapid study rhythm, rich bilingual content, lesson
control, audio practice, and flexible input—while improving:

- scheduler, queue, persistence, recovery, and undo correctness;
- separation of learning state from view/platform state;
- accessibility and keyboard parity;
- discoverability and progressive disclosure;
- responsiveness with large real packages;
- safe content/media handling;
- testable, replaceable subsystems instead of one activity-sized controller.

Success is not feature parity. It is a smaller coherent set of learner outcomes, delivered on
top of stronger learning and data-integrity foundations.
